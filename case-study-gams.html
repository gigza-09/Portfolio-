Designing Visualisations for Group Activity Metrics

Contextual Insights from older adult walking communities across India

The Problem

India is projected to have 230 million older adults by 2036, highlighting the growing need for healthy ageing.

  1. Healthy aging is inclusion of: Regular physical activities
    Appropriate Diet & Nutrition
    Preventive Health Check ups
  2. Walking is easy to start
    No equipment needed.
  3. Most fitness trackers primarily focus on individual step counts.
  4. Socializing strongly motivates walking
    (Gadahad et al.)

So, What are GAMs?

Group Activity Metric which transform individual walking numbers into shared group progress

Through Visualizations + Social Sharing

How are they designed?

  • Balance
  • Clarity
  • Facilitation
GAMs Group Activity Metrics (Gadahad et. al., CHI 2026)

What we learnt about GAMs

Fit everyday life

Some women did not carry phones while walking. Their clothes had no pockets. This affected step tracking.

Expect different comfort with phones

Comfort with the phone varied even among the oldest participants.

?

Make syncing easy

Some participants forgot to open the app. They needed a reminder call.

Use familiar images

Familiar visualisations appeared to raise interest. If the visualisations were too complicated the participants disengaged.

From Individual steps to shared experiences

Limitations

This is a small qualitative study. Each site saw different visualisations. We recruited people we already knew. We moderated the groups in our own areas. Our presence as moderators may have affected the accountability in the groups. The app had sync issues. Some people did not carry their phones while walking.

Conclusion

Overall, the study suggests that shared visualisations and social accountability may support walking habits.

  • Shared progress can spark curiosity and conversation.
  • The group itself can create accountability.
  • Design has to fit people’s daily lives.
